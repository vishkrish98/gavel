Gavel

Decision support for a student-government funding committee

Built for MGT 409, AI Foundations for Managers — Yale SOM

Introduce Gavel: it reads a club-funding policy and every request against it, and drafts a decision a human still has to approve.

The problem

  • Funding decisions are inconsistent — same facts, different clubs, different outcomes
  • Manual review is slow: one reviewer, one request at a time, against a long policy
  • Violations can be hidden in a request's own text and missed by a tired reader
Three concrete pains: committees disagree with themselves across clubs, review is slow and manual, and a request can bury text meant to mislead the reviewer.

The agent team

CHAIR is plain code, not an LLM agent, orchestrating five agents plus two deterministic, code-only steps -- Engine and Optimizer never touch a model, so a verdict or a dollar amount can't come from an agent's own words. Two human approval gates: one per rule, one per memo.

The app

This is the committee's actual screen: one row per line item, sortable, with the verdict and citation right there -- nothing to dig for in a separate document.

Limits and next steps

  • Policy and all 24 requests are synthetic — no real committee has used Gavel yet
  • The Skeptic has never caught a real error in any run (0% observed precision) — its value is unproven here
  • Instruction-pattern detection is a narrow, 5-phrase list — a differently-worded injection could reach the model unfiltered
  • No real-policy portability check, no concurrency testing, one browser tested
